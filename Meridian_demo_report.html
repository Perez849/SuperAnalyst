Valuation · method

How $280.05 of fair value becomes a $298.01 target

Several independent answers to "what is a share worth?", weighted by what fits the business, filtered by explicit guardrails.

Priced like the sell-side prices it: a target multiple on forward earnings and EBITDA (peer-anchored, adjusted for growth and earnings durability), cross-checked against a 10-year FCFF DCF whose growth fades and whose margins mean-revert. The profile for DEMO is Standard (forward multiples + DCF); it prescribes four methods, of which three produced a usable value.

The lenses, one by one

Discounted cash flow (FCFF) ($243.06 a share, 39% of the weight) values ten years of free cash flow to the firm at a 9.10% WACC with 2.50% terminal growth; the terminal value is $201.96bn (55% of EV). Growth fades toward the terminal rate and margins mean-revert, so it does not extrapolate today's profitability; here it sits below the multiples lenses.

Forward P/E ($299.27 a share, 33% of the weight) applies a 21.7x target multiple to NTM EPS of $13.77. The multiple starts from an anchor of 22.7x (60% peer median 23.8x, n=5 + 40% Industrial equipment reference 21.0x), is scaled by 0.96 for durable growth (8.8% durable growth vs 10.0% sector).

Forward EV/EBITDA ($308.77 a share, 28% of the weight) capitalises NTM EBITDA of $31.17bn at 15.0x, then bridges to equity through net cash. The anchor is 15.7x; growth and durability adjustments follow the same logic as the P/E lens, which makes it a capital-structure-neutral cross-check.

What the dispersion tells us. The lenses span $243.06 to $308.77, a 1.3x spread (coefficient of variation 10%). Agreement among independent methods is the strongest evidence a valuation can offer; here it is broad.

Weights. The profile starts from DCF 35%, forward P/E 30%, forward EV/EBITDA 25% and dividend discount model 10%. After the guardrails and the lenses that could not be built are removed, the weights are renormalised over those that remain, which is why they differ from the profile's starting point.

Guardrails: what the model is not allowed to do

Every intervention is explicit. A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded (or winsorised if it is the only one); a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is confined to 0.4×–2.5× the price; and with fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, the company is published NOT RATED rather than given a number we cannot defend.

  • Target anchored 30% to the Street consensus ($285.00, 24 analysts); model-only target $303.58.

From fair value to target

Fair value today $280.05 × (1 + 9.26% cost of equity) − $2.40 dividends = $303.58 model target; the Street mean of $285.00 at 30% weight moves it to $298.01.

Valuation · relative

What the multiples say, and what we adjust before using them

Sector and peer anchors, scaled for growth, leverage and the durability of the earnings they are applied to.

On the numbers the market pays today, Meridian Industrials (illustrative) looks in line on earnings (19.0x NTM against 21.0x for industrial equipment, −9%) and in line on enterprise value (12.8x EV/EBITDA against 14.0x).

Where the anchor comes from

The relative lenses are anchored on a curated set of 5 peers: 21.0x P/E, 14.0x EV/EBITDA, 2.3x EV/Sales and 5.0x P/B, with a sector growth rate of 10%. A sector median is a starting point, not a verdict: each lens then adjusts it for the company in front of us.

What we adjust, and why

Growth. The anchor is scaled ×0.96 (8.8% durable growth vs 10.0% sector) — the market pays for growth that lasts, so we use durable earnings growth of 8.8% (historical revenue CAGR, capped at 15%) rather than a one-year consensus jump.

The result is a target P/E of 21.7x applied to NTM EPS, worth $299.27 a share.

Cross-checks

EV/Sales is 4.3x against 2.3x for the sector, price-to-book is 10.6x against 5.0x, free-cash-flow yield is 5.0%, dividend yield is 0.9% and the PEG ratio is 2.16x. These do not enter the target unless a lens is built on them, but they show whether the headline multiple is flattered by one line of the accounts.

Growth-adjusted view. The PEG ratio of 2.16x divides the P/E by durable growth; below 1 the market is paying less than a point of multiple per point of growth. Treat it with caution when growth is a cyclical rebound rather than a trend.

P/E versus EV/EBITDA. The two multiples disagree when capital structures differ. With net cash, enterprise value is lower than market capitalisation, so EV/EBITDA flatters the shares slightly relative to P/E. We use both for that reason.

Relative valuation is only as good as the earnings it is applied to. When forward EPS is far above trailing, a low P/E can be a warning sign rather than a bargain: it is the market declining to capitalise a peak. That is why the financial analysis that follows tests the earnings base before the cash-flow model is built.

What each lever is worth

DCF: the assumptions behind the cash flows

Consensus for the near years, then a steady growth fade, mean-reverting margins and reinvestment tied to growth and returns.

The DCF is the cross-check that ignores what other investors pay. It values ten years of unlevered free cash flow plus a terminal value, discounted at the 9.10% WACC. The early years follow consensus; thereafter growth fades toward the terminal rate and margins mean-revert, so the model cannot extrapolate a single strong year.

The drivers

Revenue growth: 7.0% → 2.5% over 10y. Year 1 follows consensus revenue for FY2026. Growth then fades linearly from 7.0% to the 2.5% terminal rate as revenue compounds toward $137bn — competitive entry and the law of large numbers compress incremental growth; no single year's step is ever extrapolated.

EBIT margin: 28.5% → 28.7% → 28.3% (compresses). Reported margin is 28.5%; year-1 margin is 28.7%. This is a compounder, so we revert only 30% of the way toward the through-cycle average of 27.3%, landing at 28.3% — crediting some but not all of current profitability as durable.

Reinvestment (capex): 4.8% → 5.7% of sales. Starts at the historical capex intensity (4.8%) against D&A of 5.2%, converging to 1.10x D&A by the terminal year — a steady state that funds growth without permanent over- or under-investment.

Working capital: 16.5% of Δrevenue. Incremental working capital absorbs 16.5% of each year's revenue increase (historical median) — a cash drag as the business scales.

Tax rate: 19.0%. Normalised to the trailing effective rate of 19.0%, applied to EBIT to derive NOPAT.

Terminal growth: 2.5%. At or below long-run nominal GDP (4.0%); a mature going concern cannot outgrow the economy in perpetuity. Terminal reinvestment is g/ROIC.

Cost of capital

The cost of equity is 9.26%: a 4.3% risk-free rate plus a beta of 1.08 times a 4.6% equity risk premium. The beta is blended: 50% Blume-adjusted regression, 50% industry bottom-up. Debt costs 4.9% before tax (AAA, EBIT/int = 44.5x), 4.0% after tax, and makes up 2.9% of the capital structure at market values, so the WACC is 9.10%.

Marginal tax rate set to trailing effective (19.0%).

Regression beta 1.12 -> Blume 1.08; industry beta 1.08 (Industrial equipment, re-levered at D/E 0.03).

Terminal value

Terminal growth is 2.5%, at or below long-run nominal GDP, with terminal reinvestment of g ÷ ROIC (5% of NOPAT at a 52% terminal ROIC). The terminal value is 55% of enterprise value.

Conventions. Cash flows are discounted with a mid-year convention (flows arrive on average halfway through each year), net debt and minorities are deducted at the latest balance sheet, and the horizon is ten years for this profile. Macro context · Reloj de inversión

Where the cycle is: Stagflation, weak signal

Growth and inflation read from 28 FRED series, expressed as probabilities of four phases rather than a single label.

The Reloj de inversión — our own cycle model, published as a separate project — classifies the US economy each month into one of four phases from growth and inflation. Its latest reading (2026-10) is Stagflation (the Reloj's “Estanflación”) with weak conviction: 37% against 36% for Overheat.

How the Reloj reads the cycle

Twenty-eight FRED series are converted to robust z-scores and combined by principal components into a growth axis and an inflation axis; the month's position on that plane, and its uncertainty, give a probability for each phase. A phase is Recovery (growth above trend, inflation below), Overheat (both above), Stagflation (growth below, inflation above) or Reflation (both below).

The current reading

Growth sits at −0.01σ and inflation at +0.19σ of their ten-year norms, i.e. close to the centre of the plane, where phase calls are least reliable. Over three months growth has moved −0.04σ and inflation −0.07σ. The model's 12-month recession probability is 8%.

Why a weak signal changes how we use it

Only 1.1 percentage points separate the first and second phase. When the call is that close, naming one phase and acting on it would be false precision; the more honest estimate is the probability-weighted one. That is what we use on the next page: each phase's historical sector return, weighted by today's phase probabilities.

What each phase has favoured

The Reloj's rotation playbook, built from the same history (inverse volatility), leans on different sleeves in each phase:

  • Recovery: Consumer staples (22%), Health care (21%) and Materials (20%)
  • Overheat: Consumer staples (42%), Financials (34%) and Energy (24%)
  • Stagflation: Utilities (32%), Industrials (26%) and Semiconductors (22%)
  • Reflation: Gold miners (20%), Consumer staples (18%) and Health care (18%)

How long in this phase. The Reloj has called Stagflation for three consecutive months, and for 68% of the last 8 years. A phase that has just changed carries more uncertainty than one that has persisted.

The macro read is context for the valuation. It does not move our price target, but it does tell us whether the sector a company belongs to has historically had the wind at its back or in its face.

Direction of travel. A year ago the Reloj plotted at growth −0.56σ and inflation +0.34σ; it now plots at −0.01σ and +0.19σ, a drift toward stronger growth and little change in inflation.

Key takeaways
  • Stagflation, weak signal: 37% vs 36% for Overheat.
  • Growth −0.01σ, inflation +0.19σ; 12-month recession probability 8%.
  • We weight phases by probability; the label alone is not a forecast.

Appendix B

Methodology, definitions and disclosures

Method in one paragraph

The price target triangulates relative lenses (target multiples on consensus forward numbers, anchored on peers or a sector reference table and adjusted for durable growth, leverage and earnings durability), a 10-year DCF (consensus for the near years, a steady growth fade, mean-reverting margins) and sector models (residual income, justified P/B, FFO/AFFO, dividends, mid-cycle EBITDA), weighted by company profile. Fair value is rolled forward at the cost of equity and reduced by dividends to give a 12-month target.

Guardrails

A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded; a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is limited to 0.4×–2.5× the price; fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, is published NOT RATED. The Street consensus is anchored at up to 30% (scaled by analyst count; 15% when the count is unknown) and is always shown separately.

Rating bands

On the 12-month expected total return: OVERWEIGHT above +15%, ADD +5% to +15%, NEUTRAL −5% to +5%, REDUCE −15% to −5%, UNDERWEIGHT below −15%.

Scorecard scales

Valuation: expected return −30% to +50% mapped to 0–100. Growth: durable EPS growth 0–25% and FY+1 revenue −5% to +30%. Profitability: ROIC − WACC from −5 to +25 points and EBIT margin 0–35%. Earnings quality: Piotroski share of tests passed, OCF/net income 0.5–1.2 and accruals. Balance sheet: net debt/EBITDA, interest cover and Altman zone. Cycle fit: Reloj tilt −4 to +4 pp a year.

Quality screens

Piotroski F: nine binary tests of profitability, leverage/liquidity and efficiency; tests that cannot be computed from the available statement lines are excluded, not failed. Altman Z (original industrial form): above 2.99 safe, 1.81–2.99 grey, below 1.81 distress. Sloan accruals: (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average assets.

Reloj de inversión

Phase probabilities and sector evidence come from the Reloj de inversión project's published data (github.com/Perez849/reloj-inversion). Tilt = Σ phase probability × shrunk excess return of the sector in that phase. It describes historical behaviour and is not an input to the price target.

Data and limits

Fundamentals and consensus come from DEMO (synthetic); the company was classified as Standard (forward multiples + DCF). Forward numbers are analyst consensus where available and reported figures otherwise. A single third-party source can contain restatements, one-off items or classification differences that move the output; the model flags what it can detect and refuses to publish a number it cannot defend.

Definitions

EV: enterprise value = market capitalisation + net debt + minorities. NTM: next twelve months, a time-weighted blend of the two nearest forecast fiscal years. FCFF: free cash flow to the firm = NOPAT + D&A − capex − change in working capital. WACC: weighted average cost of capital. ROIC: NOPAT ÷ invested capital (debt + equity − cash). PEG: P/E ÷ durable earnings growth. Tilt: probability-weighted excess return of a sector against the US market, in percentage points a year.
