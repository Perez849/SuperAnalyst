Valuation · method

How $3.37 of fair value becomes a value target

Several independent answers to "what is a share worth?", weighted by what fits the business, filtered by explicit guardrails.

Spot margins mislead at both ends of the cycle: value on mid-cycle EBITDA and through-cycle multiples, with forward multiples (scaled for earnings durability) and a mean-reverting DCF as cross-checks. The profile for UEC is Cyclical / Commodities (mid-cycle multiples + DCF); it prescribes four methods, of which two produced a usable value.

The lenses, one by one

Forward EV/Sales ($2.18 a share, 50% of the weight) applies 10.1x to NTM revenue of $0.08bn (+25% vs last year): the lens for companies whose earnings do not yet measure value.

Price / Book ($4.55 a share, 50% of the weight) scales a 3.5x book multiple by returns on equity relative to the cost of equity (×0.60, ROE −8.9%).

Mid-cycle normalized EV/EBITDA was excluded (implausible: 0.1x the current price).

What the dispersion tells us. The lenses span $2.18 to $4.55, a 2.1x spread (coefficient of variation 35%). The spread is wide enough that the answer depends on which question is asked: a multiples lens capitalises today's consensus, while the DCF has to earn it. We report the disagreement rather than average it away.

Weights. The profile starts from mid-cycle EBITDA 30%, forward EV/EBITDA 25%, forward P/E 15% and DCF 30%. After the guardrails and the lenses that could not be built are removed, the weights are renormalised over those that remain, which is why they differ from the profile's starting point.

Guardrails: what the model is not allowed to do

Every intervention is explicit. A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded (or winsorised if it is the only one); a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is confined to 0.4×–2.5× the price; and with fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, the company is published NOT RATED rather than given a number we cannot defend.

Macro context · Reloj de inversión

Where the cycle is: Stagflation, weak signal

Growth and inflation read from 28 FRED series, expressed as probabilities of four phases rather than a single label.

The Reloj de inversión — our own cycle model, published as a separate project — classifies the US economy each month into one of four phases from growth and inflation. Its latest reading (2026-10) is Stagflation (the Reloj's “Estanflación”) with weak conviction: 37% against 36% for Overheat.

How the Reloj reads the cycle

Twenty-eight FRED series are converted to robust z-scores and combined by principal components into a growth axis and an inflation axis; the month's position on that plane, and its uncertainty, give a probability for each phase. A phase is Recovery (growth above trend, inflation below), Overheat (both above), Stagflation (growth below, inflation above) or Reflation (both below).

The current reading

Growth sits at −0.01σ and inflation at +0.19σ of their ten-year norms, i.e. close to the centre of the plane, where phase calls are least reliable. Over three months growth has moved −0.04σ and inflation −0.07σ. The model's 12-month recession probability is 8%.

Why a weak signal changes how we use it

Only 1.1 percentage points separate the first and second phase. When the call is that close, naming one phase and acting on it would be false precision; the more honest estimate is the probability-weighted one. That is what we use on the next page: each phase's historical sector return, weighted by today's phase probabilities.

What each phase has favoured

The Reloj's rotation playbook, built from the same history (inverse volatility), leans on different sleeves in each phase:

  • Recovery: Consumer staples (22%), Health care (21%) and Materials (20%)
  • Overheat: Consumer staples (42%), Financials (34%) and Energy (24%)
  • Stagflation: Utilities (32%), Industrials (26%) and Semiconductors (22%)
  • Reflation: Gold miners (20%), Consumer staples (18%) and Health care (18%)

How long in this phase. The Reloj has called Stagflation for three consecutive months, and for 68% of the last 8 years. A phase that has just changed carries more uncertainty than one that has persisted.

The macro read is context for the valuation. It does not move our price target, but it does tell us whether the sector a company belongs to has historically had the wind at its back or in its face.

Direction of travel. A year ago the Reloj plotted at growth −0.56σ and inflation +0.34σ; it now plots at −0.01σ and +0.19σ, a drift toward stronger growth and little change in inflation.

Key takeaways
  • Stagflation, weak signal: 37% vs 36% for Overheat.
  • Growth −0.01σ, inflation +0.19σ; 12-month recession probability 8%.
  • We weight phases by probability; the label alone is not a forecast.

Macro context · sector evidence

What the Energy sleeve has paid in each phase, and where that leaves us

Excess returns against the US market by phase, shrunk toward zero, with the statistics that say how much to trust them.

We map Uranium Energy Corp. to the Reloj's Energy sleeve, which it tracks through IYE and XLE; the Reloj's evidence for Energy runs back to 1970 and covers 226 months in its longest phase.

What the sector has paid in each phase

  • Recovery: 9.3% a year on average, +0.3 pp a year versus the US market (t = +0.1, hit rate 58%, no-signal evidence)
  • Overheat: 23.0% a year on average, +8.9 pp a year versus the US market (t = +2.4, hit rate 63%, moderate evidence)
  • Stagflation (current): 1.1% a year on average, −5.4 pp a year versus the US market (t = −1.6, hit rate 49%, weak evidence)
  • Reflation: 8.6% a year on average, −0.2 pp a year versus the US market (t = −0.1, hit rate 55%, no-signal evidence)

Read these with the statistics in mind. A t-statistic smaller than about 2 in absolute value means the sector's return in that phase cannot be told from chance; across all sector × phase cells the Reloj tests, only 25 of 192 survive false-discovery control. That is why the Reloj shrinks every estimate toward zero, and why we quote the shrunk figure.

The probability-weighted tilt

Weighting each phase's shrunk excess return by today's phase probabilities gives a tilt of +1.2 pp a year for Energy against the US market. That is small relative to the noise in the estimates: a nudge, not a thesis.

Appendix B

Methodology, definitions and disclosures

Method in one paragraph

The price target triangulates relative lenses (target multiples on consensus forward numbers, anchored on peers or a sector reference table and adjusted for durable growth, leverage and earnings durability), a 10-year DCF (consensus for the near years, geometric growth decay, mean-reverting margins) and sector models (residual income, justified P/B, FFO/AFFO, dividends, mid-cycle EBITDA), weighted by company profile. Fair value is rolled forward at the cost of equity and reduced by dividends to give a 12-month target.

Guardrails

A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded; a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is limited to 0.4×–2.5× the price; fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, is published NOT RATED. The Street consensus is anchored at up to 30% (scaled by analyst count; 15% when the count is unknown) and is always shown separately.

Rating bands

On the 12-month expected total return: OVERWEIGHT above +15%, ADD +5% to +15%, NEUTRAL −5% to +5%, REDUCE −15% to −5%, UNDERWEIGHT below −15%.

Scorecard scales

Valuation: expected return −30% to +50% mapped to 0–100. Growth: durable EPS growth 0–25% and FY+1 revenue −5% to +30%. Profitability: ROIC − WACC from −5 to +25 points and EBIT margin 0–35%. Earnings quality: Piotroski share of tests passed, OCF/net income 0.5–1.2 and accruals. Balance sheet: net debt/EBITDA, interest cover and Altman zone. Cycle fit: Reloj tilt −4 to +4 pp a year.

Quality screens

Piotroski F: nine binary tests of profitability, leverage/liquidity and efficiency; tests that cannot be computed from the available statement lines are excluded, not failed. Altman Z (original industrial form): above 2.99 safe, 1.81–2.99 grey, below 1.81 distress. Sloan accruals: (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average assets.

Reloj de inversión

Phase probabilities and sector evidence come from the Reloj de inversión project's published data (github.com/Perez849/reloj-inversion). Tilt = Σ phase probability × shrunk excess return of the sector in that phase. It describes historical behaviour and is not an input to the price target.

Data and limits

Fundamentals and consensus come from the Yahoo snapshot as of 2026-07-08; the company was classified as Cyclical / Commodities (mid-cycle multiples + DCF). Forward numbers are analyst consensus where available and reported figures otherwise. A single third-party source can contain restatements, one-off items or classification differences that move the output; the model flags what it can detect and refuses to publish a number it cannot defend.

Definitions

EV: enterprise value = market capitalisation + net debt + minorities. NTM: next twelve months, a time-weighted blend of the two nearest forecast fiscal years. FCFF: free cash flow to the firm = NOPAT + D&A − capex − change in working capital. WACC: weighted average cost of capital. ROIC: NOPAT ÷ invested capital (debt + equity − cash). PEG: P/E ÷ durable earnings growth. Tilt: probability-weighted excess return of a sector against the US market, in percentage points a year.
