Valuation · method

How $62.11 of fair value becomes a $74.06 target

Several independent answers to "what is a share worth?", weighted by what fits the business, filtered by explicit guardrails.

Regulated, low-growth, high-payout: forward P/E against regulated peers, a multi-stage dividend model, and a DCF with rate-base-style reinvestment. The profile for SO is Utility (P/E + dividends + DCF); it prescribes four methods, of which four produced a usable value.

The lenses, one by one

Forward P/E ($68.73 a share, 30% of the weight) applies a 14.4x target multiple to NTM EPS of $4.76. The multiple starts from an anchor of 17.0x (Regulated utilities reference 17.0x, no usable peer set), is scaled by 1.00 for durable growth (6.0% durable growth vs 6.0% sector).

Discounted cash flow (FCFF) ($31.02 a share, 25% of the weight) values ten years of free cash flow to the firm at a 6.96% WACC with 2.50% terminal growth; the terminal value is $72.46bn (65% of EV). Growth decays geometrically and margins mean-revert, so it does not extrapolate today's profitability; here it sits below the multiples lenses.

Multi-stage dividend discount ($62.83 a share, 25% of the weight): dividends grow from the retention-implied rate and fade to a sustainable terminal growth, discounted at the cost of equity.

Forward EV/EBITDA ($90.15 a share, 20% of the weight) capitalises NTM EBITDA of $16.27bn at 11.0x, then bridges to equity through net debt. The anchor is 11.0x; growth and durability adjustments follow the same logic as the P/E lens, which makes it a capital-structure-neutral cross-check.

What the dispersion tells us. The lenses span $31.02 to $90.15, a 2.9x spread (coefficient of variation 34%). The spread is wide enough that the answer depends on which question is asked: a multiples lens capitalises today's consensus, while the DCF has to earn it. We report the disagreement rather than average it away.

Weights. The profile starts from forward P/E 30%, dividend discount model 25%, DCF 25% and forward EV/EBITDA 20%. After the guardrails and the lenses that could not be built are removed, the weights are renormalised over those that remain, which is why they differ from the profile's starting point.

Guardrails: what the model is not allowed to do

Every intervention is explicit. A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded (or winsorised if it is the only one); a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is confined to 0.4×–2.5× the price; and with fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, the company is published NOT RATED rather than given a number we cannot defend.

  • Target anchored 30% to the Street consensus ($98.13, 19 analysts); model-only target $63.74.

From fair value to target

Fair value today $62.11 × (1 + 7.52% cost of equity) − $3.04 dividends = $63.74 model target; the Street mean of $98.13 at 30% weight moves it to $74.06.

Valuation · relative

What the multiples say, and what we adjust before using them

Sector and peer anchors, scaled for growth, leverage and the durability of the earnings they are applied to.

On the numbers the market pays today, The Southern Company looks in line on earnings (18.5x NTM against 17.0x for regulated utilities, +9%) and in line on enterprise value (10.9x EV/EBITDA against 11.0x).

Where the anchor comes from

There is no usable peer set here (fewer than three peers with valid multiples), so the relative lenses are anchored on the regulated utilities reference table: 17.0x P/E, 11.0x EV/EBITDA, 2.8x EV/Sales and 1.9x P/B, with a sector growth rate of 6%. A sector median is a starting point, not a verdict: each lens then adjusts it for the company in front of us.

What we adjust, and why

Growth. The anchor is scaled ×1.00 (6.0% durable growth vs 6.0% sector) — the market pays for growth that lasts, so we use durable earnings growth of 6.0% (consensus FY+2 / FY+1 EPS step; capped at sector growth, cyclical) rather than a one-year consensus jump.

Leverage. Net debt of 5.1x EBITDA trims the multiple by 0.85.

The result is a target P/E of 14.4x applied to NTM EPS, worth $68.73 a share.

Cross-checks

EV/Sales is 5.5x against 2.8x for the sector, price-to-book is 3.1x against 1.9x, free-cash-flow yield is −2.9%, dividend yield is 3.5% and the PEG ratio is 3.08x. These do not enter the target unless a lens is built on them, but they show whether the headline multiple is flattered by one line of the accounts.

Growth-adjusted view. The PEG ratio of 3.08x divides the P/E by durable growth; below 1 the market is paying less than a point of multiple per point of growth. Treat it with caution when growth is a cyclical rebound rather than a trend.

P/E versus EV/EBITDA. The two multiples disagree when capital structures differ. With net debt of 5.1x EBITDA, enterprise value is higher than market capitalisation, so EV/EBITDA is the harsher test. We use both for that reason.

Relative valuation is only as good as the earnings it is applied to. When forward EPS is far above trailing, a low P/E can be a warning sign rather than a bargain: it is the market declining to capitalise a peak. That is why the financial analysis that follows tests the earnings base before the cash-flow model is built.

How sensitive is the DCF?

Across a ±150bps range of WACC and ±100bps of terminal growth the DCF value per share runs from $10.61 to $77.11 (Exhibit 24). The cost of capital matters more than terminal growth: each 75bps of WACC moves the value by about $12.39 (40%), against $0.31 for 50bps of terminal growth.

Risk and reward

Measured from $88.03, the bull case adds $2.68 (+3%) and the bear case costs $31.85 (−36%): an upside-to-downside ratio of 0.1x. Asymmetry of that kind is what a rating should be built on, provided the bear case is credible.

Stress tests

Shocking one input at a time to the DCF, the most damaging are WACC +100bps (−46%) and EBIT margin −300bps (−42%); the most helpful are WACC −100bps (+62%) and EBIT margin +300bps (+42%) (Exhibit 26). How sensitive is the multiple-based value?

Forward earnings and the target multiple are the inputs that matter most. A 10% change in either NTM EPS or the target multiple moves the forward P/E value by about $6.85 a share (they enter multiplicatively); across the grid (Exhibit 25) the value runs from $43.83 to $98.62.

What each lever is worth

  • Cost of capital: every 75bps of WACC moves fair value by ≈$12.39/share (40%). Rate path and the equity risk premium are the single biggest swing factor.

  • Operating margin: each 100bps of EBIT margin across the forecast is worth ≈$4.35/share — watch pricing power, mix and cost discipline in quarterly prints.

Macro context · Reloj de inversión

Where the cycle is: Stagflation, weak signal

Growth and inflation read from 28 FRED series, expressed as probabilities of four phases rather than a single label.

The Reloj de inversión — our own cycle model, published as a separate project — classifies the US economy each month into one of four phases from growth and inflation. Its latest reading (2026-10) is Stagflation (the Reloj's “Estanflación”) with weak conviction: 37% against 36% for Overheat.

How the Reloj reads the cycle

Twenty-eight FRED series are converted to robust z-scores and combined by principal components into a growth axis and an inflation axis; the month's position on that plane, and its uncertainty, give a probability for each phase. A phase is Recovery (growth above trend, inflation below), Overheat (both above), Stagflation (growth below, inflation above) or Reflation (both below).

Appendix B

Methodology, definitions and disclosures

Method in one paragraph

The price target triangulates relative lenses (target multiples on consensus forward numbers, anchored on peers or a sector reference table and adjusted for durable growth, leverage and earnings durability), a 10-year DCF (consensus for the near years, geometric growth decay, mean-reverting margins) and sector models (residual income, justified P/B, FFO/AFFO, dividends, mid-cycle EBITDA), weighted by company profile. Fair value is rolled forward at the cost of equity and reduced by dividends to give a 12-month target.

Guardrails

A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded; a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is limited to 0.4×–2.5× the price; fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, is published NOT RATED. The Street consensus is anchored at up to 30% (scaled by analyst count; 15% when the count is unknown) and is always shown separately.

Rating bands

On the 12-month expected total return: OVERWEIGHT above +15%, ADD +5% to +15%, NEUTRAL −5% to +5%, REDUCE −15% to −5%, UNDERWEIGHT below −15%.

Scorecard scales

Valuation: expected return −30% to +50% mapped to 0–100. Growth: durable EPS growth 0–25% and FY+1 revenue −5% to +30%. Profitability: ROIC − WACC from −5 to +25 points and EBIT margin 0–35%. Earnings quality: Piotroski share of tests passed, OCF/net income 0.5–1.2 and accruals. Balance sheet: net debt/EBITDA, interest cover and Altman zone. Cycle fit: Reloj tilt −4 to +4 pp a year.

Quality screens

Piotroski F: nine binary tests of profitability, leverage/liquidity and efficiency; tests that cannot be computed from the available statement lines are excluded, not failed. Altman Z (original industrial form): above 2.99 safe, 1.81–2.99 grey, below 1.81 distress. Sloan accruals: (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average assets.

Reloj de inversión

Phase probabilities and sector evidence come from the Reloj de inversión project's published data (github.com/Perez849/reloj-inversion). Tilt = Σ phase probability × shrunk excess return of the sector in that phase. It describes historical behaviour and is not an input to the price target.

Data and limits

Fundamentals and consensus come from yfinance; the company was classified as Utility (P/E + dividends + DCF). Forward numbers are analyst consensus where available and reported figures otherwise. A single third-party source can contain restatements, one-off items or classification differences that move the output; the model flags what it can detect and refuses to publish a number it cannot defend.

Definitions

EV: enterprise value = market capitalisation + net debt + minorities. NTM: next twelve months, a time-weighted blend of the two nearest forecast fiscal years. FCFF: free cash flow to the firm = NOPAT + D&A − capex − change in working capital. WACC: weighted average cost of capital. ROIC: NOPAT ÷ invested capital (debt + equity − cash). PEG: P/E ÷ durable earnings growth. Tilt: probability-weighted excess return of a sector against the US market, in percentage points a year.
