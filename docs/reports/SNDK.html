Valuation · method

How $1,126.32 of fair value becomes a $1,343.66 target

Several independent answers to "what is a share worth?", weighted by what fits the business, filtered by explicit guardrails.

Priced like the sell-side prices it: a target multiple on forward earnings and EBITDA (peer-anchored, adjusted for growth and earnings durability), cross-checked against a 10-year FCFF DCF whose growth decays and whose margins mean-revert. The profile for SNDK is Standard (forward multiples + DCF); it prescribes four methods, of which three produced a usable value.

The lenses, one by one

Forward P/E ($1,387.82 a share, 43% of the weight) applies a 10.2x target multiple to NTM EPS of $135.44. The multiple starts from an anchor of 17.0x (Hardware & storage reference 17.0x, no usable peer set), is scaled by 1.00 for durable growth (9.0% durable growth vs 9.0% sector) and by 0.60 for earnings durability.

Forward EV/EBITDA ($1,164.91 a share, 36% of the weight) capitalises NTM EBITDA of $26.10bn at 6.6x, then bridges to equity through net debt. The anchor is 11.0x; growth and durability adjustments follow the same logic as the P/E lens, which makes it a capital-structure-neutral cross-check.

Discounted cash flow (FCFF) ($497.03 a share, 20% of the weight) values ten years of free cash flow to the firm at a 9.35% WACC with 2.50% terminal growth; the terminal value is $23.86bn (32% of EV). Growth decays geometrically and margins mean-revert, so it does not extrapolate today's profitability; here it sits below the multiples lenses.

What the dispersion tells us. The lenses span $497.03 to $1,387.82, a 2.8x spread (coefficient of variation 37%). The spread is wide enough that the answer depends on which question is asked: a multiples lens capitalises today's consensus, while the DCF has to earn it. We report the disagreement rather than average it away.

Weights. The profile starts from DCF 35%, forward P/E 30%, forward EV/EBITDA 25% and dividend discount model 10%. After the guardrails and the lenses that could not be built are removed, the weights are renormalised over those that remain, which is why they differ from the profile's starting point.

Guardrails: what the model is not allowed to do

Every intervention is explicit. A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded (or winsorised if it is the only one); a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is confined to 0.4×–2.5× the price; and with fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, the company is published NOT RATED rather than given a number we cannot defend.

Valuation · relative

What the multiples say, and what we adjust before using them

Sector and peer anchors, scaled for growth, leverage and the durability of the earnings they are applied to.

On the numbers the market pays today, Sandisk Corporation looks cheap on earnings (12.8x NTM against 17.0x for hardware & storage, −25%) and cheap on enterprise value (9.8x EV/EBITDA against 11.0x).

Where the anchor comes from

There is no usable peer set here (fewer than three peers with valid multiples), so the relative lenses are anchored on the hardware & storage reference table: 17.0x P/E, 11.0x EV/EBITDA, 2.5x EV/Sales and 5.0x P/B, with a sector growth rate of 9%. A sector median is a starting point, not a verdict: each lens then adjusts it for the company in front of us.

What we adjust, and why

Growth. The anchor is scaled ×1.00 (9.0% durable growth vs 9.0% sector) — the market pays for growth that lasts, so we use durable earnings growth of 9.0% (historical revenue CAGR, capped at 15%; capped at sector growth, cyclical) rather than a one-year consensus jump.

Earnings durability. Forward EPS of $135.44 is 20.0x the through-cycle EPS of −$10.24, so the target multiple is scaled by ×0.60. This is the single largest adjustment and the main reason our target sits below consensus.

The result is a target P/E of 10.2x applied to NTM EPS, worth $1,387.82 a share.

Cross-checks

EV/Sales is 7.6x against 2.5x for the sector, price-to-book is 27.8x against 5.0x, free-cash-flow yield is 0.0% and the PEG ratio is 1.42x. These do not enter the target unless a lens is built on them, but they show whether the headline multiple is flattered by one line of the accounts.

Growth-adjusted view. The PEG ratio of 1.42x divides the P/E by durable growth; below 1 the market is paying less than a point of multiple per point of growth. Treat it with caution when growth is a cyclical rebound rather than a trend.

Relative valuation is only as good as the earnings it is applied to. When forward EPS is far above trailing, a low P/E can be a warning sign rather than a bargain: it is the market declining to capitalise a peak. That is why the financial analysis that follows tests the earnings base before the cash-flow model is built.

Piotroski F-score

The score counts nine binary tests of profitability, leverage and efficiency. Sandisk Corporation passes six — positive operating cash flow, cash flow exceeds net income (low accruals), current ratio improving, no dilution (share count not up) and two more; it fails three: positive return on assets, return on assets improving and leverage falling.

The failures are worth reading in context: failing the level tests means the business is not yet earning a positive return on its assets or generating operating cash, which is the failure that matters most.

  • Profitability: 2 of 4 passed
  • Leverage and liquidity: 2 of 3 passed
  • Efficiency: 2 of 2 passed

Distress risk

Altman's Z-score combines working capital, retained earnings, operating profit, market value of equity relative to liabilities and sales to assets. At 41.6 Sandisk Corporation sits in the safe zone (above 2.99 is safe, below 1.81 is distress). How the Reloj reads the cycle

Twenty-eight FRED series are converted to robust z-scores and combined by principal components into a growth axis and an inflation axis; the month's position on that plane, and its uncertainty, give a probability for each phase. A phase is Recovery (growth above trend, inflation below), Overheat (both above), Stagflation (growth below, inflation above) or Reflation (both below).

The current reading

Growth sits at −0.01σ and inflation at +0.19σ of their ten-year norms, i.e. close to the centre of the plane, where phase calls are least reliable. Over three months growth has moved −0.04σ and inflation −0.07σ. The model's 12-month recession probability is 8%.

Why a weak signal changes how we use it

Only 1.1 percentage points separate the first and second phase. When the call is that close, naming one phase and acting on it would be false precision; the more honest estimate is the probability-weighted one. Appendix B

Methodology, definitions and disclosures

Method in one paragraph

The price target triangulates relative lenses (target multiples on consensus forward numbers, anchored on peers or a sector reference table and adjusted for durable growth, leverage and earnings durability), a 10-year DCF (consensus for the near years, geometric growth decay, mean-reverting margins) and sector models (residual income, justified P/B, FFO/AFFO, dividends, mid-cycle EBITDA), weighted by company profile. Fair value is rolled forward at the cost of equity and reduced by dividends to give a 12-month target.

Guardrails

A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded; a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is limited to 0.4×–2.5× the price; fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, is published NOT RATED. The Street consensus is anchored at up to 30% (scaled by analyst count; 15% when the count is unknown) and is always shown separately.

Rating bands

On the 12-month expected total return: OVERWEIGHT above +15%, ADD +5% to +15%, NEUTRAL −5% to +5%, REDUCE −15% to −5%, UNDERWEIGHT below −15%.

Scorecard scales

Valuation: expected return −30% to +50% mapped to 0–100. Growth: durable EPS growth 0–25% and FY+1 revenue −5% to +30%. Profitability: ROIC − WACC from −5 to +25 points and EBIT margin 0–35%. Earnings quality: Piotroski share of tests passed, OCF/net income 0.5–1.2 and accruals. Balance sheet: net debt/EBITDA, interest cover and Altman zone. Cycle fit: Reloj tilt −4 to +4 pp a year.

Quality screens

Piotroski F: nine binary tests of profitability, leverage/liquidity and efficiency; tests that cannot be computed from the available statement lines are excluded, not failed. Altman Z (original industrial form): above 2.99 safe, 1.81–2.99 grey, below 1.81 distress. Sloan accruals: (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average assets.

Reloj de inversión

Phase probabilities and sector evidence come from the Reloj de inversión project's published data (github.com/Perez849/reloj-inversion). Tilt = Σ phase probability × shrunk excess return of the sector in that phase. It describes historical behaviour and is not an input to the price target.

Data and limits

Fundamentals and consensus come from the Yahoo snapshot as of 2026-07-09; the company was classified as Standard (forward multiples + DCF). Forward numbers are analyst consensus where available and reported figures otherwise. A single third-party source can contain restatements, one-off items or classification differences that move the output; the model flags what it can detect and refuses to publish a number it cannot defend.

Definitions

EV: enterprise value = market capitalisation + net debt + minorities. NTM: next twelve months, a time-weighted blend of the two nearest forecast fiscal years. FCFF: free cash flow to the firm = NOPAT + D&A − capex − change in working capital. WACC: weighted average cost of capital. ROIC: NOPAT ÷ invested capital (debt + equity − cash). PEG: P/E ÷ durable earnings growth. Tilt: probability-weighted excess return of a sector against the US market, in percentage points a year.
