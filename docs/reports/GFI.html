Valuation · method

How $54.62 of fair value becomes a $58.38 target

Several independent answers to "what is a share worth?", weighted by what fits the business, filtered by explicit guardrails.

Spot margins mislead at both ends of the cycle: value on mid-cycle EBITDA and through-cycle multiples, with forward multiples (scaled for earnings durability) and a mean-reverting DCF as cross-checks. The profile for GFI is Cyclical / Commodities (mid-cycle multiples + DCF); it prescribes four methods, of which four produced a usable value.

The lenses, one by one

Discounted cash flow (FCFF) ($87.04 a share, 30% of the weight) values ten years of free cash flow to the firm at a 7.71% WACC with 2.50% terminal growth; the terminal value is $46.81bn (59% of EV). Growth decays geometrically and margins mean-revert, so it does not extrapolate today's profitability; here it sits above the multiples lenses.

Mid-cycle normalized EV/EBITDA ($26.56 a share, 30% of the weight): commodity spot margins mislead at cycle peaks and troughs; this normalises to the through-cycle average margin and multiple.

Forward EV/EBITDA ($50.06 a share, 25% of the weight) capitalises NTM EBITDA of $8.99bn at 5.2x, then bridges to equity through net debt. The anchor is 6.5x; growth and durability adjustments follow the same logic as the P/E lens, which makes it a capital-structure-neutral cross-check.

Forward P/E ($53.52 a share, 15% of the weight) applies a 9.5x target multiple to NTM EPS of $5.61. The multiple starts from an anchor of 12.0x (Precious metals reference 12.0x, no usable peer set), is scaled by 1.00 for durable growth (6.0% durable growth vs 6.0% sector) and by 0.79 for earnings durability.

What the dispersion tells us. The lenses span $26.56 to $87.04, a 3.3x spread (coefficient of variation 40%). The spread is wide enough that the answer depends on which question is asked: a multiples lens capitalises today's consensus, while the DCF has to earn it. We report the disagreement rather than average it away.

Weights. The profile starts from mid-cycle EBITDA 30%, forward EV/EBITDA 25%, forward P/E 15% and DCF 30%. After the guardrails and the lenses that could not be built are removed, the weights are renormalised over those that remain, which is why they differ from the profile's starting point.

Guardrails: what the model is not allowed to do

Every intervention is explicit. A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded (or winsorised if it is the only one); a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is confined to 0.4×–2.5× the price; and with fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, the company is published NOT RATED rather than given a number we cannot defend.

Valuation · relative

What the multiples say, and what we adjust before using them

Sector and peer anchors, scaled for growth, leverage and the durability of the earnings they are applied to.

On the numbers the market pays today, Gold Fields Limited looks cheap on earnings (5.9x NTM against 12.0x for precious metals, −51%) and cheap on enterprise value (3.5x EV/EBITDA against 6.5x).

Where the anchor comes from

There is no usable peer set here (fewer than three peers with valid multiples), so the relative lenses are anchored on the precious metals reference table: 12.0x P/E, 6.5x EV/EBITDA, 3.2x EV/Sales and 2.2x P/B, with a sector growth rate of 6%. A sector median is a starting point, not a verdict: each lens then adjusts it for the company in front of us.

What we adjust, and why

Growth. The anchor is scaled ×1.00 (6.0% durable growth vs 6.0% sector) — the market pays for growth that lasts, so we use durable earnings growth of 6.0% (historical revenue CAGR, capped at 15%; capped at sector growth, cyclical) rather than a one-year consensus jump.

Earnings durability. Forward EPS of $5.61 is 3.2x the through-cycle EPS of $1.74, so the target multiple is scaled by ×0.79. This is the single largest adjustment and the main reason our target sits away from consensus.

The result is a target P/E of 9.5x applied to NTM EPS, worth $53.52 a share.

Cross-checks

EV/Sales is 2.8x against 3.2x for the sector, price-to-book is 3.6x against 2.2x, free-cash-flow yield is 8.1% and the PEG ratio is 0.98x. These do not enter the target unless a lens is built on them, but they show whether the headline multiple is flattered by one line of the accounts.

Growth-adjusted view. The PEG ratio of 0.98x divides the P/E by durable growth; below 1 the market is paying less than a point of multiple per point of growth. Treat it with caution when growth is a cyclical rebound rather than a trend.

P/E versus EV/EBITDA. The two multiples disagree when capital structures differ. With net debt of 0.2x EBITDA, enterprise value is higher than market capitalisation, so EV/EBITDA is the harsher test. We use both for that reason.

Relative valuation is only as good as the earnings it is applied to. When forward EPS is far above trailing, a low P/E can be a warning sign rather than a bargain: it is the market declining to capitalise a peak. That is why the financial analysis that follows tests the earnings base before the cash-flow model is built.

How sensitive is the DCF?

Across a ±150bps range of WACC and ±100bps of terminal growth the DCF value per share runs from $64.35 to $149.05 (Exhibit 23). The cost of capital matters more than terminal growth: each 75bps of WACC moves the value by about $12.40 (14%), against $4.37 for 50bps of terminal growth.

Risk and reward

The price sits below even our bear case ($49.55), which means every scenario we run is above today's price. We would treat that as a prompt to test whether the bear case is conservative enough rather than as free money.

Stress tests

Shocking one input at a time to the DCF, the most damaging are WACC +100bps (−16%) and revenue growth 20% lower (−9%); the most helpful are WACC −100bps (+23%) and revenue growth 20% higher (+10%) (Exhibit 25). Only WACC −100bps lifts the DCF to the blended fair value on its own.

How sensitive is the multiple-based value?

Forward earnings and the target multiple are the inputs that matter most. A 10% change in either NTM EPS or the target multiple moves the forward P/E value by about $5.33 a share (they enter multiplicatively); across the grid (Exhibit 24) the value runs from $34.11 to $76.74.

What each lever is worth

  • Cost of capital: every 75bps of WACC moves fair value by ≈$12.40/share (14%). Rate path and the equity risk premium are the single biggest swing factor.

  • Top-line delivery: our base assumes 25.0% year-one growth fading to 2.5%; sustained beats/misses versus that glide path are the cleanest re-rating trigger either way.

DCF: the assumptions behind the cash flows

Consensus for the near years, then geometric growth decay, mean-reverting margins and reinvestment tied to growth and returns.

The drivers

Revenue growth: 25.0% → 2.5% over 10y. Year 1 follows consensus revenue for FY2026. Growth then decays geometrically from 8.0% to the 2.5% terminal rate as revenue compounds toward $15bn — competitive entry and the law of large numbers compress incremental growth; no single year's step is ever extrapolated.

EBIT margin: 50.3% → 71.7% → 52.2% (expands). Reported margin is 50.3%; year-1 margin is 71.7%. This is a cyclical business, so we revert 60% of the way toward the through-cycle average of 39.2%, landing at 52.2% — crediting some but not all of current profitability as durable.

Reinvestment (capex): 21.8% → 16.5% of sales. Starts at the historical capex intensity (21.8%) against D&A of 15.0%, converging to 1.10x D&A by the terminal year — a steady state that funds growth without permanent over- or under-investment.

Working capital: −3.6% of Δrevenue. Incremental working capital releases 3.6% of each year's revenue increase (historical median) — a source of cash (negative working-capital model).

Tax rate: 35.0%. Normalised to the trailing effective rate of 35.0%, applied to EBIT to derive NOPAT.

Terminal growth: 2.5%. At or below long-run nominal GDP (4.0%); a mature going concern cannot outgrow the economy in perpetuity. Terminal reinvestment is g/ROIC.

Cost of capital

The cost of equity is 8.20%: a 4.3% risk-free rate plus a beta of 0.85 times a 4.6% equity risk premium. The beta is blended: 50% Blume-adjusted regression, 50% industry bottom-up. Debt costs 4.9% before tax (AAA, EBIT/int = 36.5x), 3.2% after tax, and makes up 9.9% of the capital structure at market values, so the WACC is 7.71%.

Marginal tax rate set to trailing effective (35.0%).

Regression beta 0.52 -> Blume 0.68; industry beta 1.02 (Precious metals, re-levered at D/E 0.11).

Terminal value

Terminal growth is 2.5%, at or below long-run nominal GDP, with terminal reinvestment of g ÷ ROIC (8% of NOPAT at a 30% terminal ROIC). The terminal value is 59% of enterprise value.

Conventions. Cash flows are discounted with a mid-year convention (flows arrive on average halfway through each year), net debt and minorities are deducted at the latest balance sheet, and the horizon is ten years for this profile. How the Reloj reads the cycle

Twenty-eight FRED series are converted to robust z-scores and combined by principal components into a growth axis and an inflation axis; the month's position on that plane, and its uncertainty, give a probability for each phase. A phase is Recovery (growth above trend, inflation below), Overheat (both above), Stagflation (growth below, inflation above) or Reflation (both below).

Growth sits at −0.01σ and inflation at +0.19σ of their ten-year norms, i.e. close to the centre of the plane, where phase calls are least reliable. Over three months growth has moved −0.04σ and inflation −0.07σ. The model's 12-month recession probability is 8%.

Appendix B

Methodology, definitions and disclosures

Method in one paragraph

The price target triangulates relative lenses (target multiples on consensus forward numbers, anchored on peers or a sector reference table and adjusted for durable growth, leverage and earnings durability), a 10-year DCF (consensus for the near years, geometric growth decay, mean-reverting margins) and sector models (residual income, justified P/B, FFO/AFFO, dividends, mid-cycle EBITDA), weighted by company profile. Fair value is rolled forward at the cost of equity and reduced by dividends to give a 12-month target.

Guardrails

A lens outside 0.2×–5× the share price is excluded; a lens more than 2.2× away from the median of the others is down-weighted by 60%; the published target is limited to 0.4×–2.5× the price; fewer than two usable lenses, or a result forced by a guardrail, is published NOT RATED. The Street consensus is anchored at up to 30% (scaled by analyst count; 15% when the count is unknown) and is always shown separately.

Rating bands

On the 12-month expected total return: OVERWEIGHT above +15%, ADD +5% to +15%, NEUTRAL −5% to +5%, REDUCE −15% to −5%, UNDERWEIGHT below −15%.

Scorecard scales

Valuation: expected return −30% to +50% mapped to 0–100. Growth: durable EPS growth 0–25% and FY+1 revenue −5% to +30%. Profitability: ROIC − WACC from −5 to +25 points and EBIT margin 0–35%. Earnings quality: Piotroski share of tests passed, OCF/net income 0.5–1.2 and accruals. Balance sheet: net debt/EBITDA, interest cover and Altman zone. Cycle fit: Reloj tilt −4 to +4 pp a year.

Quality screens

Piotroski F: nine binary tests of profitability, leverage/liquidity and efficiency; tests that cannot be computed from the available statement lines are excluded, not failed. Altman Z (original industrial form): above 2.99 safe, 1.81–2.99 grey, below 1.81 distress. Sloan accruals: (net income − operating cash flow) ÷ average assets.

Reloj de inversión

Phase probabilities and sector evidence come from the Reloj de inversión project's published data (github.com/Perez849/reloj-inversion). Tilt = Σ phase probability × shrunk excess return of the sector in that phase. It describes historical behaviour and is not an input to the price target.

Data and limits

Fundamentals and consensus come from the Yahoo snapshot as of 2026-07-08; the company was classified as Cyclical / Commodities (mid-cycle multiples + DCF). Forward numbers are analyst consensus where available and reported figures otherwise. A single third-party source can contain restatements, one-off items or classification differences that move the output; the model flags what it can detect and refuses to publish a number it cannot defend.

Definitions

EV: enterprise value = market capitalisation + net debt + minorities. NTM: next twelve months, a time-weighted blend of the two nearest forecast fiscal years. FCFF: free cash flow to the firm = NOPAT + D&A − capex − change in working capital. WACC: weighted average cost of capital. ROIC: NOPAT ÷ invested capital (debt + equity − cash). PEG: P/E ÷ durable earnings growth. Tilt: probability-weighted excess return of a sector against the US market, in percentage points a year.
